The logistic map xₙ₊₁ = r·xₙ·(1−xₙ) is a population model. At low r, the population settles to one stable value. As r increases, it bifurcates — the population oscillates between two values, then four, then eight. The doublings accelerate, then collapse into chaos.

But inside the chaos, pockets of order appear — islands of period-3, period-5, period-7 cycles, then chaos again. This is the structure of complexity: not random, not predictable, but governed by a deterministic rule that the future cannot be read from. The butterfly effect was first seen not in a storm, but in a diagram like this one.
