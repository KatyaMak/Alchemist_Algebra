How Evidence Updates Belief · The Art of Conditional Reasoning

The Question
A person screens positive. What is the actual probability they have depression?
P(condition | positive test)
Posterior Probability
53%
A positive screening result raises the probability from 20% to 53%. The evidence moved the belief — but uncertainty remains significant.
Prior — Before Evidence 20%
Posterior — After Positive Test 53%
False Positive Rate 20%
✦
The Calculation · Bayes' Theorem
P(condition | positive) = P(positive | condition) × P(condition) / P(positive)

= 0.85 × 0.20 / 0.32 = 0.53
The prior was 20% — the base rate of depression in this population. The positive screening result is strong evidence, but not conclusive. Bayes forces you to account for how common the condition actually is. A rare condition with a positive test can still have a low posterior probability — because most positives are false alarms. This is why base rates matter in clinical decision-making.
