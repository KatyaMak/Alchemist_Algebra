A vector's coordinates are its shadows cast onto the axes — always shorter than the vector itself. When you multiply those shadows and sum them, the result is the dot product. And because shadows cannot outrun the object casting them, the dot product can never exceed the product of the two lengths. This is Cauchy-Schwarz — not a formula imposed from outside, but a geometric fact the space refuses to violate.

Watch what happens when the vectors point the same direction: the dot product reaches |a||b|, the bar fills completely, and the angle is zero. When they are perpendicular, the dot product is zero — the vectors are completely silent to each other. Perpendicularity, in the language of algebra, means dot product equals zero.
